# ALEA — three works, endless editions

An exhibition of generative works written in alea, a language where a program
describes a family of artworks and every run draws a new one: an *edition*.
Each image here is one edition, named by its seed. The same seed draws the
same image again, exactly; any other seed draws a work no one has seen.

## Second room: Correspondances

A new work, posted after the opening, or in Nocturne's place (posts 2 and 5).

| # | Post | Files |

| 8 | Correspondances, Reel (20 s, a day of service) | `8-correspondances-reel.mp4` (cover: `8-correspondances-reel-cover.jpg`) |

**How it is made.** The interchanges sit near where they really are
(Étoile, Saint-Lazare, Châtelet, République, Nation…), jittered and snapped to
the plan's grid. Each of the fourteen lines goes through the interchanges it
really serves, but between two of them it runs as a schematic map draws it:
a diagonal and a straight stretch, the order chosen by chance. Under the
network: the Seine with its two islands, the ring road, the woods of Boulogne
and Vincennes, and the arrondissements as a faint spiral. In the Reel, `t` is
one day: no trains at night, the first ones at dawn, all of them at the rush
hours, a chime for each arrival.

## Third room: Culture

| # | Post | Files |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Culture, carousel of 6 | `9-culture-*.jpg` |
| 10 | Culture, Reel (20 s, 72 hours of growth) | `10-culture-reel.mp4` (cover: `10-culture-reel-cover.jpg`) |

**How it is made.** Culture is the first work that uses `grow`: fields with a
memory, computed generation by generation on a grid of 256 × 256 cells. Two
chemicals, food and life, react and spread for 6000 generations (a Gray–Scott
reaction–diffusion). A few drops of life are seeded at random in the dish. The
chemistry differs across the dish: two regimes are drawn for each edition
(cells that divide, coral, maze, fingerprint, holes, spots) and blended along
a map of noise, so two kinds of growth meet. A third grown field keeps a
faint stain wherever life has been. The light is computed from the slope of
the living layer, as on a wet surface.

### 9 · Culture

**EN**

fingerprints. The chemistry changes across the dish, so two kinds of life
grow side by side and meet. Nothing here is drawn; all of it is grown.

Editions 0xc00 · 0xc05 · 0xc06 · 0xc0b · 0xc0a · 0xc01
Written in alea.

**FR**

Éditions 0xc00 · 0xc05 · 0xc06 · 0xc0b · 0xc0a · 0xc01

## Fourth room: Kikai

| # | Post | Files |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | Kikai, carousel of 6 | `12-kikai-*-0x*.jpg` (0xa1ea05 · 0xa1ea04 · 0xa1ea08 · 0xa1ea01 · 0xa1ea0a · 0xa1ea0f) |
| 12 | Kikai, Reel (15 s, eight bars at 128 BPM) | `12-kikai-reel.mp4` (cover: `12-kikai-reel-cover.jpg`) |

**How it is made.** The drawing is the still, and every motion is zero at
`t = 0`, so the print is the Reel's first frame. `t` is eight bars of four
beats: the kick is a pulse that rises in a frame and fades, and the speakers
swell with it, the VU needle swings with a slower one, the reels turn at two
speeds, the step lamp runs over the sixteen pads. The track is written in alea
too, as synths: a drum machine (a sine that falls in pitch for the kick,
filtered noise for the claps and hats) and a bass synthesizer, a saw through a
resonant filter, the acid line. That line is one bar drawn for each edition,
and it is drawn under the machine, step by step, as it plays. In the last four
bars come the stabs of Detroit, a minor ninth and a major ninth; in the last two
beats the kick stops and the picture turns to negative, once.

### 12 · Kikai (Reel)

**EN**

KIKAI, in motion. A rhythm machine that never existed plays a track that
never existed either: four on the floor at 128 BPM, an acid line drawn
by chance (the one written under the machine is the one you hear), and in the
second half the minor chords of Detroit. Drawn, composed and synthesized by
the same program. Edition 0xa1ea05.

**FR**

KIKAI, en mouvement. Une boîte à rythmes qui n'a jamais existé joue un
morceau qui n'a jamais existé non plus : quatre temps à 128 BPM, une ligne

## Fifteenth room: Couches

| # | Post | Files |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | Couches, Reel (130 s, a loop), explained by a voice | `24-couches-reel.mp4` (cover: `24-couches-reel-cover.jpg`) |

**How it is made.** Requête's sibling: how a network works, in nine steps
and ten works in `couches/`, each a diagram narrated by the same voice
(`say` through `voices/kokoro.py`, `af_heart`), its words typed under the
step header as it speaks, a stack of the seven OSI layers at the top right
lighting the one we are in. *Protocol* (two machines, a wire that carries
only a signal, two messages crossing it, and a sheet of rules written line
by line: who speaks first, what a message is made of), *Model* (the seven
layers rising on two machines, physical to application, then a dashed line
from each to its twin across the way: the same protocol at each end),
*Wrap* (encapsulation: a block of data going down the five layers of the
stack, a header sliding in front of it at each one, TCP, IP, the frame's
addresses and a trailer, until at the bottom it is a row of bits; the same
work with `up: 1` is the other end, where every header is peeled off
again, bottom to top), *App* (HTTP's request and answer, DNS turning a name
into an address, and the others with their jobs), *Transport* (a message cut
into six numbered segments, each flying from sender to receiver and
acknowledged with a dot back, one lost half way and sent again; under it
UDP's stream that never waits), *Network* (a packet with its two IP
addresses, carried router to router along a route across a scattered map,
its TTL counting down), *Link* (one cable, four machines with their MAC
addresses, ARP shouting along it, the right card answering, then the frame
with its two MACs in front and a checksum behind), *Physical* (the same
bits as a square wave on copper, pulses of light in a fibre, a radio wave
whose amplitude follows them) and *End* (seven layers, one message). The
stages take the time the voice needs, with a breath between sentences;
each is painted in its own colour and keeps its own sound under the voice:
tones for the hellos, keys for the rules, a note for each layer rising, a
thud at every step down the stack and up again, keys and blips for the
applications, blips for the segments and their acknowledgements, a low
thud for the lost one, pulses at every hop, a burst for ARP's shout, the
modem's song under the bits, a chord when the message arrives whole, and a
clock ticking under everything. The addresses are the ones kept for
documentation (192.0.2.44, 203.0.113.7, locally administered MACs):
nobody's.

## Sixteenth room: Brut

| # | Post | Files |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | Brut, Reel (60 s), from a phone clip | `25-brut-reel.mp4` (cover: `25-brut-reel-cover.jpg`) |

**How it is made.** The first work with a video as input. An eight-second
phone clip of a DJ in the basement (not in the repository) goes through
`brut/make-inputs.py`: 126 of its frames are laid out on sheets
(`brut/sheets/hi0–6.jpg`, eighteen 640 × 1138 frames each, and `lo.jpg`,
all of them at 256 × 455), and its sound is chopped by `brut/chop.py` into
`brut/chop.wav`. The sheets are the trick: a `sequence` of 126 pictures
costs 126 lookups at every pixel, a frame picked out of a sheet with one
`picture` (its `x`, `y` moved by the frame's index, a field) costs one, so a
scene can show a different frame in every cell, strip or sixteenth.
`brut/gen.py` writes `brut.alea`: ten scenes on a 128 bpm grid (12 or 16
beats each, 32 bars, 60 s), every index of the picture driven by `hash` of
the current sixteenth, eighth or beat, so nothing holds still. *Strobe* (a
random still every sixteenth, posterised black and white, some inverted,
some black, the globe flickering on the eighths), *Tear* (the clip at its
own speed, red, green and blue pulled apart on every kick, slices torn
sideways), *Grid* (sixteen cells, each on its own frame, one of them the
globe, flickering on the eighths, a few in heat), *Kaleidoscope* (the
orange record mirrored four, six and eight ways, posterised in the hot
palette, pumping with the kick), *Globe* (the clip seen through a disc with
the globe turning over it and the wordmark flickering under, then the disc
cut out of the clip), *Melt* (the break: the clip running backwards through
warped space, bloom and chromatic aberration), *Lines* (edges and
scanlines, pixelated on some eighths, a flash of the colour frame on each
kick), *Ghosts* (two moments of the clip, their difference, so only what
moved shows, inverted on some beats, tiled on others), *Strips* (eight
columns, each stuttering between its own two frames, some blacked out) and
*Out* (the clip clean, alternating with the stamp, the globe and the
wordmark on black, then black). The cuts are `glitch` transitions, one
`pixels`, one `jump` into the break. The sound is the clip's own, reworked:
`chop.py` cuts it into sixteenth-note slices (the loud ones more often) and a
sequencer fires them on the grid, each through pitch (half speed, double,
backwards), a gate envelope, 1/64 stutters, a lowpass with a slow LFO, a
bitcrusher on some bars and a dotted-eighth delay, with a granular drone an
octave down under the breaks; alea plays the result as a `sound:` layer.
Under it a track in the Detroit way: 808 synths written as `synth` blocks
(a kick with a pitch drop and a click, a three-burst clap, closed and open
hats, a cowbell of two squares), a bass that never sits still (root, fifth
and octave on a sixteenth pattern, through a resonant lowpass whose cutoff
follows an envelope), stabs in three voices on a progression (i i VI VII in
A minor, by `chords` and `harmony:`), an `arp` of plucks and a lead of
chorused squares on the minor pentatonic, drawn from the edition's seed, on
32 bars of `steps` patterns that build, break at bar 16 and come back.

**How it is made.** An invitation, in a minute of cuts on a 120 BPM grid:
